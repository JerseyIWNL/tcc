Emergency department overcrowding is worsened by low-acuity patients, and combining self-service kiosks with telemedicine may offer these patients an alternative channel inside the health unit itself. This work proposes and analytically assesses a software architecture for kiosk-based telemedicine, aimed at post-triage care of patients classified as green or blue by the Manchester Triage System in Brazilian public health units. The research is applied and follows the Design Science Research paradigm, combining a targeted narrative review, traceable requirements derivation, layered modelling with the C4 model, and a formative ex ante analytical evaluation carried out by the author through quality scenarios, regulatory conformance checking and consistency and coverage checks of the traceability matrix. The result is a five-layer architecture: kiosk, teleconsultation platform, clinical backend, HL7 FHIR R4 gateway to the National Health Data Network and the primary care record system, and cross-cutting security. The proposal includes a traceability matrix with 19 requirements, clinical criteria for eligibility, reassessment and return to in-person care, patient identification through a code issued at triage, a mapping of the sections of the national Clinical Encounter Record, a specification of authentication, validation and error handling based on the network's official documentation, a distinction between the legal basis for data processing and clinical consent, and privacy, contingency and accessibility controls. In this self-assessment, 25 of 39 conformance items were considered met at design level, 9 partially met and 5 not verifiable without a prototype, certification or testing in the network's certification environment; of ten scenarios, five were met at design level, one partially, and four depend on clinical validation, field measurement, certification testing or user studies. The normative coverage check showed that six regulatory obligations, all organizational, were not translated into system requirements. This is a preliminary proposal, neither prototyped nor empirically evaluated. Its compatibility is design-level and depends on expert validation, workflow simulation, certification testing and cost and performance analysis.
